1. Purpose & North Star

This document defines concrete, engineering-buildable metric formulas for the Rider Reliability System, refining the high-level targets in PRD §2 — Goals & Success Metrics into definitions that map onto real, verified schema fields. It does not change product scope or the Sprint 1 / Sprint 2 split — it answers "what exactly do we compute, and can we actually compute it?"

North Star: "Make unreliability cheap to recover from." Not "eliminate no-shows." The metric set below is deliberately weighted toward recovery, because that is both what the product is for and what the data can honestly support.

Pillar 1 — Detect risk early

Via structured T-12 / T-2 WhatsApp confirmation. Measured by the Detection Metrics in §3.1.

Pillar 2 — Cut replacement time

Via reserve rider + standby bench. Measured by the Recovery Metrics in §3.2. This pillar now carries the headline number.

The one structural constraint

Everything in this document follows from a single fact about the system:

Scheduled Instant records rider commitments, not delivery outcomes. The module has no link to the delivery/dispatch side of the platform, and no attendance or arrival signal of any kind. It knows what a rider said they would do, and what ops did about it. It never learns whether the delivery happened.

Any metric that requires proof a delivery occurred is therefore not buildable in this version — see §5. Any metric built from commitments and ops actions is buildable, needs no manual bookkeeping, and is what §3 contains.

2.2 A5 resolved — the enum is exactly as the PRD states

✅ Verified in src/database/schema/enum/si-confirmation-status.enum.ts. All seven values match the PRD §5 table verbatim: PENDING, CONFIRMED, NO_RESPONSE, NEEDS_REPLACEMENT, REPLACED, COMPLETED, FAILED. The canonical schema xlsx was stale and should be corrected. The systemic rework risk flagged in v1.0 is closed — no metric needs redefinition on this account.

2.3 A7 resolved — status is mutable, but fully historized elsewhere

confirmation_status is overwritten in place. However, v1.0's concern that this loses history is unfounded, because two append-only logs capture every transition:

  • schedule_slot_activity — every manual ops change, with from_status, to_status, actor, reason, created_at.
  • schedule_wa_messages — every WhatsApp message, with direction, checkpoint (T12/T2), button payload (YA/TIDAK) and timestamp.

The T-12-versus-T-2 answer is fully recoverable. Metrics that need checkpoint-level detail read the message log, not the status column.

Why this kills v1.0's headline metric. Unrecovered Failure Rate was defined as FAILED ÷ (COMPLETED + FAILED). Both sides only exist if an ops associate opens each finished slot and manually marks it. Nothing does this automatically. In practice ops record exceptions and skip routine successes — so the ratio is not merely noisy, it is biased upward, and would report a crisis in a good month. With per-rider granularity (confirmed 06-08) that is up to 450 manual clicks a day. See §5. Unaffected by the 07-08-2026 fixes below — this is a separate, still-open structural gap.

3. Metric Catalog

Why the recovery metrics are the trustworthy ones. Replacing a rider is something ops genuinely do inside the product — open the slot, search standby, assign. Ops phone the reserve rider outside the system, so the call is not recorded anywhere — only the resulting assignment is. Measuring "called" would require a new logging step in Slot Detail.

3.3 Reading rules — mandatory for correctness

RuleWhy
Count role = 'utama' only. Exclude cadangan rows from every metric and from the per-slot readiness column. Reserve riders are seeded onto every slot and never receive a T-12 ping, so they sit at PENDING permanently. Including them makes every slot look bad and would manufacture large numbers of phantom no-shows.
Exclude REPLACED rows from rate denominators where the replacement carries the outcome. Otherwise a successful recovery is counted twice — once as the rider who dropped out, once as the rider who covered.
Per-rider, not per-slot (confirmed 06-08-2026). A 15-rider slot contributes 15 rows. Note the consequence: large-roster clients dominate the global average, which is why the per-client table matters as much as the headline.
Suppress cells below 5 slots in the per-client table; show n/a. Small denominators produce percentages that read as fact. Already applied to Wicked Pies in the mockup.
Exclude everything before WhatsApp go-live. Not the dashboard go-live date (22-06-2026) — see §8/A3.

4. Headline vs. Diagnostic Hierarchy

v1.0 made Unrecovered Failure Rate the headline. §2.4 shows that number cannot be produced honestly. The replacement is not a downgrade — it is a closer match to the product's stated goal.

Headline — reported upward

Waktu Cari Pengganti · Time-to-Replacement
median( replacement assigned − flagged as needing replacement )

Target: under 20–30 minutes, from ~2 hours before this system existed. This target is already written into PRD §1 and §2 — it is the North Star's own number, and it is measurable today with trustworthy data.

Diagnostic — recovery layer

Berhasil Dapat Pengganti
recovered ÷ needed replacement

Is the standby bench actually working? Speed without success rate is misleading — fast recoveries on 60% of gaps is not a win.

Diagnostic — prevention layer

Nggak Konfirmasi T-12 / T-2
declined or silent ÷ pinged

Is the confirmation flow reducing no-shows at all? Rising no-shows with flat recovery time still means the underlying problem is worsening.

Why the split still matters

The reasoning from v1.0 §3 holds, and now applies to the recovery pair. A single blended number can improve for the wrong reason. Reporting recovery speed alone would look like a win even if the bench were succeeding less often; reporting success rate alone would hide a two-hour scramble behind a healthy-looking percentage. Both, side by side, name which lever moved.

A second reason to keep the YA / TIDAK / silent split. A rider who taps TIDAK gives ops a two-hour head start; a rider who goes silent costs the full timeout before anyone knows there is a gap. Both land in the same no-show percentage, but they cost very different amounts. If that mix shifts toward silence, recovery time worsens even with a flat no-show rate — and chasing riders toward any answer is a cheaper intervention than chasing them toward yes.

5. Out of Scope — and Why

Three metrics from v1.0 are formally dropped. They fail for one shared reason, stated once so this does not resurface three separate times:

Scheduled Instant records rider commitments, not delivery outcomes. Any metric requiring proof that a delivery happened is out of scope until the calendar is integrated with the delivery side of the platform.
Dropped metricWhat it needsRoute back
Unrecovered Failure Rate
"Slot Gagal Total" — v1.0's headline		 COMPLETED and FAILED, neither of which anything writes. Manual close-out was rejected: per-rider granularity makes it up to 450 clicks a day, and partial compliance biases the number upward rather than merely adding noise. Derive completion from the delivery system once slots link to real orders. Then it becomes automatic and honest.
Slot Coverage Rate
"Slot Ketutup"		 Attendance. There is no check-in, arrival event or GPS signal in this module. The nearest proxy — a rider tapping YA — is a promise, and the gap between promise and attendance is the exact problem this product exists to solve. Needs an arrival signal. A separate workstream, not a metric definition.
Client-first gap discovery A client-complaint timestamp. Confirmed absent from this codebase — complaints arrive by WhatsApp, phone or account managers. Needs somewhere for ops or AMs to log "client flagged this first". A small feature, not a derived metric.

Deferred to Sprint 2 — per-rider reliability %

The shipped Screen 8 mockup displayed a Reliabilitas XX% badge on every rider card. These are removed in the v2 mockup. Technically the aggregation is available today, but three problems stand:

  • Sample size. A rider with 2 assignments who missed 1 reads 50%, with the same visual authority as a 54-slot client average.
  • It measures the wrong thing. Built from confirmation data, it cannot distinguish "unreliable" from "doesn't tap buttons but always turns up" — the same defect that removed Slot Coverage Rate, now attached to a named individual whose livelihood depends on getting assigned.
  • The label overstates it. "Reliabilitas" implies attendance; the data is response.

If reintroduced in Sprint 2, three guardrails apply: rename to Tingkat Respons, hide below ~10 assignments in the window, and keep it out of the rider picker and roster cards where it silently shapes who gets work.

7. Required Changes

7.1 Schema additions — largely unnecessary

v1.0 §5 requested escalated_at and replaced_at on slot_riders. Do not build these.
  • t12_escalated_at and t2_escalated_at already exist on schedule_slot_riders — two stamps, one per checkpoint, which is better than the single column specced.
  • replaced_at is not needed: schedule_slot_activity already stamps the REPLACE action with created_at.
Time-to-Replacement is computable today with zero migration.
