1. Background & Problem

Scheduled Instant is a Dash Electric logistics offering where clients need a rider (mitra) to pick up at the client's hub at a scheduled time and deliver directly to the end customer. Riders are gig workers, not employees. The Scheduled Instant Squad (part of the Logistic team) manages roughly 19 in-scope clients.

The core problem is rider unreliability. Riders frequently fail to show up without warning. Because there is no early-warning signal, clients often discover the gap before the ops team does, and the manual scramble to find a replacement rider can currently take up to 2 hours — during which the client relationship is actively being damaged and no one is tracking how often this happens.

Current Situation

Ops tags riders in a WhatsApp group at H-1 as a reminder. Some respond, many leave it on read. If a rider no-shows, the client usually notices first. Ops then manually calls around for a replacement — up to 2 hours — with no structured visibility, no history, and schedules managed across fragmented per-client spreadsheets in five different formats.

Desired Outcome

Unreliability becomes cheap to recover from. Risk is detected early via structured rider confirmation, ops sees gaps before the client does, and replacement time drops from ~2 hours to under 20–30 minutes through a pre-designated reserve rider plus a standby bench — all managed in one dashboard that is the single source of truth.

North-star principle: "Make unreliability cheap to recover from" — the system does not try to eliminate no-shows (structurally impossible with a gig workforce). It detects risk early and shortens recovery time.

2. Goals & Success Metrics

Primary Goals

  • Detect rider no-show risk early via structured T-12 / T-2 WhatsApp confirmation.
  • Give ops a single source of truth (dashboard) that surfaces gaps before the client notices.
  • Cut replacement time from ~2 hours to under 20–30 minutes via reserve rider + standby bench.

Secondary Goals

  • Retire fragmented per-client spreadsheets as the write system (Model B).
  • Build a clean history of rider reliability to enable a rider-tier system in a later sprint.

8. Edge Cases

Rider Day-Off Conflict (soft-block)

If ops assigns a rider on their active day-off, the system does not hard-block. It shows a warning with the conflict reason and requires explicit confirmation to override. Riders often agree to work a nominal day off for extra pay; a hard block would fail that real scenario.

Multi-Rider Slot Statuses

A slot has 1–15 riders, each with an independent confirmation status. "Needs replacement" applies per-rider, not per-slot. Slot-level views aggregate. Reserve riders must be excluded from any aggregate percentage — they never receive a ping and would permanently depress the number.

Reserve Rider Is Not Auto-Pinged

Reserve riders receive no WhatsApp at slot creation. They are ops' first manual call if a main rider goes TIDAK or no-response. Only after ops assigns them does the reserve rider get an info-only WhatsApp — never a second YA/TIDAK loop. The phone call itself happens outside the system and is not recorded — which is why the Schedule Detail card measures reserves ditugaskan (assigned), not dipanggil (called).

Edit Slot vs. Edit Schedule

Editing a single slot occurrence never changes the recurring definition. Editing a schedule cascades to future slots only — today and the past are never touched. Riders already confirmed or manually touched are protected from roster-cascade removal.

Rider Roster Is a Point-in-Time Snapshot

Slot riders are copied from the schedule roster when the slot is generated. Later roster edits affect only slots after today. A schedule whose roster was trimmed will therefore show larger rider counts on historical slots — correct behaviour, and the explanation for the count mismatch recorded in context.md §11.1.

Escalation Timing

T-12 no-response → flagged after 2 hours. Implemented and running.
T-2 no-response → flagged after 30 minutes. ✅ Fixed 07-08-2026 — specified here and in the WhatsApp Flow mockup; now implemented and running, mirroring T-12.

Both fire a dashboard flag and a Slack post, grouped by client + slot, to the ops channel — channel-wide, no individual tagging.

12. Implementation Notes

Model B — Dashboard Is the Single Source of Truth

Client spreadsheets are retired as the write system. Ops enter and manage all slots in the dashboard. The historical migration ran once and imported schedules and rosters only — it created no slot or rider rows, so all slot history is genuine generated data.

WhatsApp Confirmation Timing Anchors to Window Start

T-12 and T-2 are computed from window_start_time, not window end. One message per rider per shift/pickup, not per delivery stop.

Metrics Computation Rules

  • Count role = 'utama' only. Reserve riders never receive a ping and sit at PENDING permanently; including them manufactures phantom no-shows and depresses every readiness percentage.
  • Exclude REPLACED rows from denominators where the replacement carries the outcome, or a successful recovery is counted twice.
  • Unit is the rider-assignment, not the slot. Large-roster clients therefore dominate global averages — the per-client table matters as much as the headline.
  • Suppress cells below 5 slots in per-client views; show n/a.
  • Attribute no-shows to a checkpoint via the escalation timestamps, not the status value — both checkpoints produce the same status.
  • Exclude data before WhatsApp go-live, not before dashboard go-live (22-06-2026).

Standby Matching Rule

The only match criterion is "no schedule conflict"; the earlier 5 km radius rule was dropped. Day-off riders are excluded from suggestions but can be assigned with explicit override. Reserve riders surface at the top of the standby list.

Rider Tag Semantics

"Dedicated Riders" = riders tagged Scheduled Instant. "All Riders" = every Dash rider except those tagged Express. Where these tags are managed is still unconfirmed.

Deprecated Concepts — Do Not Reintroduce

Single-trip / multi-trip and route_type / delivery_group_id were removed — the source column is dead and there is no operational definition. Use batch_label. Stop-count-per-route belongs to a route-planning system, not this one.
